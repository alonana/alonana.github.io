Yesterday I've tried to edit the calibration units to use micrometer instead of millimeter.
This caused the swift software to crash and delete all the calibrations.
So, today I've re-calibrated it.

Next I took another sample, but this time I took a bit deeper from the water, 
unlike the previous times when I sampled the surface.
This time I found many very small cells moving fast, but no big cells.

![sample]({{ '/assets/images/2026-10-27/a.png' | relative_url }})

Somehow disappointed with the results, I decided to try something else.
I took a toothpick and gently scrubbed around my cheek.
I have vague memories of doing a similar test on school.

![sample]({{ '/assets/images/2026-10-27/b.png' | relative_url }})

This human cells a huge.
The biggest cells I saw so far are about 4 microns, but human cells are about 100 microns.
We can see the cell membrane, the nucleus and, many organelles.
Unlike the bacteria, human cells are static. 
I cannot observe any movement, not of the cells themselves, and within them.
I am wondering if these cells are dead?

![sample]({{ '/assets/images/2026-10-27/c.png' | relative_url }})

Trying to estimate, I am guessing about 1K cell exist in the sample.
I think that to better see the different part I might need to use some coloring techniques.

In some cells I could clearly notice some relatively big organelles.
I am wondering if these are mitochondria.

![sample]({{ '/assets/images/2026-10-27/d.png' | relative_url }})


In some locations I could find small cells by the human cells.
Maybe these are bacterias?
